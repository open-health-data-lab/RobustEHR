# The assertion task: worked examples and baseline comparison

Stage 8 decides, for every clinical concept in a note, **how the note modifies
that concept's presence**. Four modifiers can apply, and the SEMANTIC operator
channel exists to flip them:

| modifier | what it does to the concept's presence | operator that flips it |
|---|---|---|
| `negated` | withholds it — the concept is absent | `semantic.negation_flip` |
| `uncertain` | qualifies it — possible, or contingent on a condition | `semantic.uncertainty_flip` |
| `historical` | relocates it in **time** — present, but in the past | `semantic.temporality_shift` |
| `family` | relocates it in **person** — present, but not in the patient | `semantic.experiencer_shift` |

*Pneumonia*, *no pneumonia*, *possible pneumonia*, *pneumonia in his father* —
one concept, four claims. Perturbing the claim without touching the concept is
what this stage makes possible.

A span carrying none of the four is **unmodified**, which ConText and this
codebase call `affirmed`. Note that only `negated` truly withholds the
assertion: *history of hypertension* affirms the hypertension and places it in
the past, and *father with MI* affirms the MI in someone else. That is why this
document says **modified** rather than *non-affirmed* — the latter is true of at
most two of the four.

This document does two things the per-dimension F1 tables cannot: it shows **what
one annotated span actually looks like**, and it puts our detector next to
**every baseline we could measure** on three separate instruments.

> **We annotated MIMIC. The quoted examples are synthetic anyway.**
>
> **Every score in this document comes from real clinical text** — 1,000
> MIMIC-IV spans across 100 discharge summaries, plus 2,365 spans of published
> NegEx gold. Nothing is measured on synthetic notes.
>
> But this file is committed, and quoting the sentence around a MIMIC span is
> exactly the disclosure the PhysioNet Credentialed Health Data Use Agreement
> forbids — de-identification does not make note text redistributable. So the
> **quotes** in §1 come from `tests/fixtures/clinical`, written from scratch and
> describing no real patient, while the **numbers** everywhere else come from
> MIMIC.
>
> The real annotated spans are not lost, only unpublishable. Running
>
> ```bash
> python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_examples.py --gold data/assertion_gold_mimic/gold.yaml \

## 1. A snapshot of annotation

Eight spans, chosen to cover the four dimensions and both outcomes. `stock` is
medspaCy ConText as distributed; `extended` adds
`resources/assertion_extensions.yaml`. The **cue** column is the trigger the arm
fired on — it is what explains a disagreement, and a blank cue means the arm saw
nothing at all.

| # | sentence (synthetic) | span | gold | detector | cue | ✓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|:-:|
| 1 | DENIES FEVER OR VOMITING | `FEVER` | `negated` | `negated` | `DENIES` | ✅ |
| 2 | …but no orthopnea or paroxysmal nocturnal… | `orthopnea` | `negated` | `negated` | `no`, `but` | ✅ |
| 3 | history of ischemic cardiomyopathy comma status post anterior myocardial | `ischemic cardiomyopathy` | `historical` | `historical` | `history` | ✅ |
| 4 | Attends daycare where several children have been unwell | `unwell` | `family` | `family` | `children` | ✅ |
| 5 | ALCOHOL ABUSE, HYPERTENSION, GERD | `HYPERTENSION` | `historical` | affirmed | — | ❌ |
| 6 | …Plan comma continue current medications comma repeat echocardiogram | `echocardiogram` | `uncertain` | affirmed | — | ❌ |
| 7 | #Dispo: Anticipate discharge tomorrow pending clinical stability | `discharge` | `uncertain` | affirmed | — | ❌ |
| 8 | Also breifly discussed shingles vaccine, pt will think about it | `shingles vaccine` | `uncertain` | affirmed | — | ❌ |

Four hits, four misses, and **the four misses share one cause**: there is no cue.

- **Row 5** is historical because of the header it sits under (`PAST MEDICAL
  HISTORY`), not because of anything in the sentence. A section prior would catch
  it. We do not use one — assertion is resolved at the span level, and a section
  header is not evidence about the span, only about its neighbourhood.
- **Rows 6–8** are contingent or planned rather than asserted: *repeat* echo,
  *anticipate* discharge, *will think about* the vaccine. Each is uncertain in
  substance and carries no negation-style trigger. Row 8 also contains a typo
  (*breifly*) — incidental here, but the kind of thing the operator channel
  exists to inject deliberately.

This is the shape of the problem on the fixtures. It was also, for a while, this
document's headline claim about the corpus — *detection, not scoping, is the
bottleneck* — and **that claim was too broad.** A per-error attribution
(`validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_error_analysis.py`) partitions every held-out miss into
*cue-absent* and *cue-present-but-unscoped*, and the answer differs by dimension:

| | TP | FP | FN, no cue | FN, cue present | bottleneck |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `negated` | 24 | **21** | 3 | 7 | **precision** — over-scoping |
| `uncertain` | 14 | 18 | 8 | **15** | **recall** — scope reach |
| `historical` | 6 | 11 | **21** | 11 | **recall** — vocabulary |

Only `historical` is a detection problem. `negated` produces almost as many false
positives as true ones, and `uncertain`'s misses mostly have the cue sitting
right there in the sentence — 33 of the 66 false negatives across the three are a
cue *after* its target, which a FORWARD-only rule can never reach at any
vocabulary size. Three dimensions, three different repairs, and the pooled F1
table cannot tell them apart.

### 1.1 What the real MIMIC spans show

The synthetic rows above are clean by construction. The 20 real annotated spans
in `data/assertion_gold_mimic/examples.md` are not, and the failures divide into
five recurring constructions. Described rather than quoted, for the reason in the
box above:

**Where the detector succeeds** — the cases matching the published algorithm's
assumptions: a negation verb or `no evidence of` preceding the finding in the
same clause; a temporal adverb such as *previously* before a treated condition; a
hedging verb (*likely*, *thought to represent*) before a diagnosis; a kinship
term introducing a relative's condition. These behave exactly as the fixtures
predict, on real notes.

**Where it fails**, in order of how much of the error mass each accounts for:

1. **Negation carried by tabular structure, not by syntax.** Laboratory result
   blocks negate by appending a token to an analyte name, or by one summary word
   governing a list of cultures. There is no cue *preceding* the finding, so a
   left-scanning algorithm has nothing to trigger on. This is the single largest
   `negated` failure mode on real notes and it is absent from both NegEx gold and
   our fixtures — a consequence of curated corpora selecting prose sentences.
2. **History carried by narrative tense.** A relapse described in past tense with
   a date, or a condition named with a post-surgical abbreviation, is historical
   to a reader and cueless to the detector. `s/p` is now in the extensions; the
   general case of past-tense narration is not solvable lexically.
3. **Uncertainty carried by disposition.** Follow-up arrangements and
   recommendations to resume something later are contingent, not asserted.
   Nothing hedges them; the construction is a plan, and the tense is what carries
   the modality. This mirrors rows 6–8 above, so the fixtures do represent this
   one faithfully.
4. **Over-scoped experiencer.** A `family` cue used in a *narrative* role rather
   than an attributive one — a relative as the person who found the patient, not
   as the bearer of a finding — scopes forward over the patient's own findings.
   This is the error the annotation guideline was corrected to expose, and it is
   still present.
5. **Cue-word collision.** A lab analyte whose name contains a negation cue as a
   substring-adjacent token pulls in a negation that belongs to a neighbouring
   result. A boundary problem, not a vocabulary one.

Failures 1 and 2 are the reason `negated` scores κ 0.56 on MIMIC against F1 0.93
on NegEx gold: **the published benchmark contains no lab tables**. Failure 3 is
why `uncertain` recall is the weakest number in this document. Failure 4 is why
`family` precision is worse than its κ suggests.

## 2. Instrument 1 — negation on the NegEx gold

The published benchmark: 2,365 annotated concept mentions from de-identified
discharge summaries and radiology reports (Chapman et al.), 20.8% negated. Single
sentences, curated, negation only.

| arm | what it is | P | R | **F1** | acc |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **negspaCy** | independent third-party NegEx | 0.9283 | 0.9491 | **0.9386** | 0.9742 |
| **ConText (ours)** | medspaCy ConText, shipped | 0.9021 | 0.9572 | **0.9289** | 0.9696 |
| dependency parse | spaCy `neg` arc over the mention | 0.9211 | 0.7128 | 0.8037 | 0.9277 |
| ±5-token window | cue anywhere in a fixed window | 0.4269 | 0.9450 | 0.5881 | 0.7252 |
| majority class | always affirmed | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.7924 |

**negspaCy wins by one F1 point, and we ship ConText anyway.** The reason is
structural and this benchmark cannot show it: negspaCy detects negation and
nothing else. ConText returns four dimensions and emits cue and scope spans,
which is what gives the operator channel a dose denominator. Choosing the
single-dimension winner would trade three dimensions and the whole SEMANTIC
channel for one point on the fourth.

The two weak arms are load-bearing, not filler. The **window** arm shows that
knowing the cue vocabulary without scope logic costs 35 F1 points. The
**majority** arm shows that 79% accuracy is what guessing achieves, which is why
nothing in this document reports accuracy as a headline.

## 3. Instrument 2 — cue detection on real notes

*Can each system find the trigger at all?* 40 held-out MIMIC discharge summaries,
scored on cue spans rather than on concepts, so scope resolution is removed and
only vocabulary is under test.

The **lexicon floor** is every medspaCy literal matched as a bare substring, with
no direction, scope or termination. The gap above it is what the engine adds; the
gap below stock is what our mined extensions add.

| dimension | lexicon floor | negspaCy | ConText stock | **ConText + ours** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `negated` | 0.6974 | 0.7350 | 0.7129 | **0.7758** |
| `uncertain` | 0.3086 | — | 0.4622 | **0.7463** |
| `historical` | 0.4026 | — | 0.4601 | **0.6975** |
| `family` | 0.3906 | — | **0.8775** | **0.8775** |

*F1 on cue spans, from `validation/measurements/cue_detection.json`. `—` = the
implementation does not attempt that dimension. An earlier version of this table
carried figures from a previous run (negated 0.8191, uncertain 0.7325, historical
0.7066); the committed artifact supersedes them.*

Three findings:

1. **Stock ConText is close to a bare keyword list on three of four
   dimensions.** For `historical` it beats the floor by 5 points and for
   `uncertain` by 15 — on real notes, most of what the stock engine contributes
   to those dimensions is the word list itself.
2. **The extensions close most of the gap.** `uncertain` +26 F1, `historical`
   +22. These were mined on the 60% development split and scored here on the
   held-out 40%, so the gain is out of sample. `prn` alone accounted for 258
   missed cues on the development notes.
3. **`family` needs nothing.** Stock ConText's kinship pattern already carries 51
   terms; there was no gap to close, and the extensions correctly add nothing.

## 4. Instrument 3 — span classification on real notes

The end-to-end task: given a concept span, which dimensions apply? 399 held-out
spans from 40 MIMIC discharge summaries, annotated by two independent LLM
annotators against a guideline written before any label was assigned.

Cohen's κ, because these classes are rare enough that raw agreement is
uninformative — a rater answering "affirmed" to everything scores 0.71 here.

| arm | `negated` | `uncertain` | `historical` | `family`ᵃ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| lexicon co-occurrence (floor) | 0.311 | 0.206 | 0.116 | 0.328 |
| negspaCy | 0.345 | — | — | — |
| ConText, PyRuSH boundaries | **0.562** | 0.270 | 0.092 | 0.663 |
| ConText, our boundaries | 0.514 | 0.221 | 0.089 | **0.748** |
| **ConText + our cues** | 0.486 | **0.334** | **0.312** | **0.748** |
| *annotator ceiling* | *0.913* | *0.890* | *0.845* | *0.899* |

ᵃ 3 gold positives — κ reported, P/R/F1 withheld as false precision.

**The rank order reverses between instrument 1 and instrument 3.** negspaCy beat
ConText by one F1 point on curated single sentences; on real multi-sentence
discharge summaries ConText beats it by 22 κ points (0.562 vs 0.345). negspaCy
predicts 79 negations where the gold has 31 — its scope runs on without the
termination rules ConText carries. **A benchmark of curated single sentences does
not predict behaviour on discharge summaries**, and that discrepancy is worth
more than either number alone.

**The extensions trade negation for the other two.** `historical` +22 κ and
`uncertain` +6 κ, against `negated` −3. The trade is deliberate: negation was
already the strongest dimension and the two weakest were the ones blocking
operators.

**Every arm sits well below the annotator ceiling**, defined next.

### 4.1 What "annotator ceiling" means here

The ceiling row is **Cohen's κ between two independent annotators on the same
1,000 spans** — `openai/gpt-5.6-luna` and `openai/gpt-5.6-terra`, each given the
identical span list and the identical guideline, which was committed before any
label was assigned. Neither saw the other's output.

It is called a ceiling because **a detector cannot be shown to agree with the
gold better than the gold agrees with itself.** Where two independent readers
disagree about whether a span is `historical`, "correct" is not defined for that
span, and any detector score computed over it is measuring the yardstick as much
as the instrument. κ 0.845 on `historical` therefore bounds what a `historical`
detector could demonstrate here even if it were perfect. This is standard
practice for annotated corpora; what is unusual is only that the annotators are
models rather than clinicians, which is stated below.

Two agreement statistics are measured, and they answer different questions:

| statistic | design | negated | uncertain | historical | family |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **inter-annotator κ** *(the ceiling)* | two different models, same spans, same guideline | **0.913** | **0.890** | **0.845** | **0.899** |
| intra-annotator κ | one model, second pass, span order reversed | 0.966 | 0.903 | 0.904 | 0.947 |

Exact label-set agreement is **0.933** between annotators and 0.958 within one.

**Only the inter-annotator figure is a ceiling.** The intra-annotator pass shows
that one rater repeats itself — which a consistently *wrong* rater also does — so
it bounds noise, not correctness. Running only the intra-annotator check would
have looked like validation and established nothing; that is why the second model
was run.

**Three caveats, because this is not a clinician-annotated corpus:**

1. **The annotators are LLMs**, so the gold is a well-agreed second opinion, not
   ground truth. Two models sharing a training distribution can agree on the same
   mistake in a way two clinicians would not, which means the ceiling is
   plausibly *optimistic*.
2. **Agreement is not accuracy.** κ 0.913 on `negated` says the two readings
   coincide, not that either matches what the clinician meant.
3. **`family` rests on 9 positives** across the full gold and 3 held out. Its
   κ 0.899 is real but fragile; one flipped span moves it materially.

What the ceiling licenses is narrow and worth stating exactly: **where two
independent annotators agree and the detector does not, the detector is the
outlier.** That is enough to rank arms and to locate errors, which is what this
document does with it. It is not enough to call any of these labels correct.

## 5. Three families of detector, on the same 400 spans

Everything above measures one detector family — a published rule algorithm and
its ablations. That was the right place to start, under this package's reuse
rule: cite an evaluated component, measure only what you change. But the rules
are near their ceiling. A cue-direction sweep bought `historical` +0.060 held
out; the scope fix the error analysis pointed straight at did not generalise at
all. ConText implements a 2001 algorithm, and the honest move is to measure the
gap to what else exists rather than assert the rules are good enough.

Three families, same held-out spans, same gold:

### 5.1 The encoder wins negation and cannot see the rest

`bvanaken/clinical-assertion-negation-bert` is Bio_Discharge_Summary_BERT
fine-tuned on the i2b2 2010 assertion challenge. It takes a sentence with the
target wrapped in `[entity]` markers and returns PRESENT / ABSENT / POSSIBLE —
**exactly this stage's seam**, spans supplied from outside.

It beats every rule arm on negation by 0.117 F1 and then stops. It predicted
POSSIBLE **once in 400 spans** and called 36 of 37 gold-`uncertain` spans
PRESENT. That is a label-semantics mismatch rather than a failure: i2b2's
`possible` is a diagnostic hedge, while this package's `uncertain` deliberately
merges hedges with conditionals, which i2b2 splits off as
`conditional`/`hypothetical` — and this checkpoint dropped both. **The merge that
made our lexicon work is what makes the checkpoint inapplicable to the
dimension.**

`historical` is worse than unsupported: **i2b2's assertion task has no temporality
class at all**, so the dimension has no published baseline in any benchmark. That
is the strongest single argument for annotating our own gold.

One disclosure travels with the encoder's numbers: its base model is pretrained
on MIMIC-III discharge summaries, and this gold is MIMIC-IV — overlapping corpora
in period and patients. Pretraining exposure, not fine-tuning leakage, but not
nothing.

### 5.2 The prompt was the largest single lever, and it was ours

The first LLM arms over-called everything: recall to 1.000, precision 0.22–0.65,
and Flash-Lite labelling 127 of 400 spans `uncertain` against a gold of 37. The
cause was not the models. **The prompt had been written to build the gold, not to
detect**, and it never told the model how rare modifiers are — so every arm
answered "could this be modified?" instead of "is it".

v2 changes nothing about the guideline, the label set, the span markup or the
JSON contract. It adds the ~70% unmodified base rate, an explicit requirement
that a cue must *govern this span* rather than appear near it, and a tie-break
toward the empty list: *a missed modifier is one error; an invented one changes
what the note says.*

| 2.5 Flash, held out | v1 | v2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `negated` F1 | 0.660 | **0.838** |
| `negated` precision | 0.493 | **0.775** |
| spans predicted negated (gold 34) | 69 | **40** |
| exact label set | 0.765 | **0.798** |

**Chosen on the development half and reported on held-out once.** Iterating
prompt wording against the test half is fitting the test half, and this
repository has already had to withdraw one number for a subtler version of
exactly that mistake. The development sweep predicted a large negation gain and
two small ones; the large gain transferred and grew, the small ones did not,
which is what one real effect and two noise effects look like.

It also does not help uniformly: Flash-Lite gained 0.05 exact-set and got *worse*
on negation. **Telling a model the base rate only helps if it can act on it.**

### 5.3 `unmodified` is the column that governs the dose

72.5% of spans carry no modifier, and until now nothing scored that class. It is
reported here because for this package it matters more than any dimension: **a
span wrongly marked modified is a span withheld from an operator**, so poor
`unmodified` precision silently shrinks the dose denominator without changing any
number an operator reports.

It is also the cleanest single summary of detector quality, because predicted
counts converge on the gold's 290 exactly as the arms improve — 196, 231, 288.
Flash-Lite calls 45% of unmodified spans modified; 3.6 Flash reaches recall 0.966
at precision 0.972.

### 5.4 Scoring above the annotator ceiling, and what was done about it

Gemini 3.6 Flash reaches κ 0.967 on negation against a gold whose two annotators
agreed at 0.936. **A detector cannot be more correct than its gold**, so that
number was checked rather than published. What a third model *can* be is more
similar to annotator 1 than annotator 2 was — which would make the figure an
artefact of shared bias rather than quality.

Scored against each annotator separately, on the same spans:

| exact label-set agreement | |
|---|---:|
| luna vs terra — the two gold annotators | 0.9525 |
| 3.6 Flash vs luna (**= the gold**) | 0.9500 |
| 3.6 Flash vs terra (**independent**) | 0.9450 |

It agrees with each annotator about as well as they agree with each other. So it
is a **third rater of equivalent quality**, not a model tracking one annotator's
idiosyncrasies — and the numbers in the table are read as such.

This is the strongest evidence available given an LLM-annotated gold, and it is
still not proof of correctness. All three models could share a bias no clinician
would, and nothing in this corpus could detect that.

### 5.5 Why the best arm cannot ship, and what does instead

Gemini 3.6 Flash clears 0.70 on all four dimensions. It will not be the shipped
detector, and the reason is not quality.

This package's central claim is **deterministic, replayable edit scripts** — the
AST lint bans `random`, `hash()` and wall-clock from `src/`, operators fold their
resources' SHA-256 into their effective version, and a run replays byte-identical
offline. An API call satisfies none of that. It is also **not cheap**: at
$1.50/$7.50 per million tokens 3.6 Flash costs five times 2.5 Flash on input, so
it answers *"does a stronger model clear the bar"*, not *"is a cheap model good
enough"*.

A fine-tuned encoder satisfies all of it. **The encoders here are nonetheless
comparison baselines rather than candidates for the pipeline** — they are the
tier a methods paper has to report between rules and LLMs, and §5.6 shows what
that tier is worth on this task. They are not wired into `qualify_document` and
are not intended to be: the LLM reads assertion better, and the shipped
deterministic detector is the rule set.

**The LLM arm is kept, not discarded.** It is reproducible from committed
scripts, its responses are cached so a re-run is offline and free, and it serves
three purposes a deterministic detector cannot: it bounds how far the shipped
detector is from what is achievable, it supplies the supervision the encoder
distils, and it is available to a user who wants maximum accuracy and can accept
that their run will not replay byte-identically. What it will not be is the
default, because the default has to keep the package's central promise.

Two caveats on the table itself. `family` carries **4 held-out positives**, so its
figures are counts wearing a metric's clothes and are omitted above. And every
LLM number is agreement with an LLM-written gold; the deterministic arms are
scored against the same gold, so the *comparison* is fair even where the absolute
level is not established.

### 5.6 Distilling the gold, and the dimension where rules still win

The gold is frontier-LLM supervision at κ 0.845–0.913. An LLM cannot sit in a
package promising replayable edit scripts; an encoder can. So the architecture
worth testing is **LLM annotates → encoder distils → deterministic detector
ships**, and `validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_finetune.py` runs it on the 600 development spans
— multi-label over four dimensions, 480 train / 120 validation notes / 400 test,
with the test half never seen during training or model selection.

**The caveat was stated before the run, not discovered after it.** Those 600
spans carry 82 `uncertain`, 61 `historical`, 47 `negated` and **5 `family`**.

| base | `negated` | `uncertain` | `historical` | `family` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| i2b2 assertion checkpoint | **0.815** | 0.518 | 0.382 | 0.00 |
| Bio_ClinicalBERT | 0.538 | **0.659** | 0.410 | 0.00 |
| BiomedBERT (PubMed only) | 0.593 | 0.434 | 0.362 | 0.29 |

**600 examples distil better than they had any right to.** Negation goes 0.725
zero-shot → **0.815** fine-tuned, clearing 0.70 on a detector that is
deterministic, offline and byte-reproducible. Uncertainty goes 0.442 from rules
→ 0.659. The architecture works.

**And rules still win `historical`.** CTX-D's 0.525 beats the best encoder's
0.410 — on the one dimension with no published baseline in any benchmark. The
evidence there is *structural*: a section header, which is layout rather than
language, and 61 training examples cannot teach a transformer to read a layout
that a five-line rule reads exactly. This is the least fashionable result in the
document and the one held most firmly.

**No single base wins.** The i2b2 checkpoint keeps its negation advantage through
fine-tuning — a model that already knows the task spends 47 examples well —
while Bio_ClinicalBERT is 0.14 better on uncertainty. A deployed deterministic
stack would be per-dimension rather than one model, which is a real cost and is
stated as one.

**The contamination control is reassuring rather than clean.** Bio_ClinicalBERT
is pretrained on MIMIC-III, which overlaps this MIMIC-IV gold in period and
patients; BiomedBERT has never seen a clinical note. The MIMIC-pretrained base
wins `uncertain` by 0.225 and `historical` by 0.048 and **loses `negated` by
0.056**. Memorisation would help uniformly; a mixed result argues domain fit. It
does not rule exposure out.

`family` is 0.00 on two bases and 0.29 on the third, from five examples. It is
reported as untrained, not as a measurement.

### 5.7 CTX-D: a header as a cue, not as a prior

21 of 32 held-out `historical` misses have no cue anywhere in the span's
sentence. No scope change reaches them; the dimension needs evidence the sentence
does not contain, and most of those spans sit under a header saying the section
is past.

A blanket section prior — *everything here is historical* — is not span-level
evidence, and this package resolves assertion at the span level. So the header is
treated as a **cue**: text in the note, with a scope, defeasible by other text.

**The data rejected the obvious version before it was built.** By section, on
development:

| section header | historical / total |
|---|---:|
| `Past Medical History` | **22/22** |
| `Past Surgical History` | **2/2** |
| `PAST ONCOLOGIC HISTORY` | 3/5 |
| `History of Present Illness` | 10/71 |
| `Brief Hospital Course` | 11/102 |

A rule keying on `history` fires on *History of Present Illness*, which is 86%
**not** historical — the HPI is the current admission's story and its header
contains the word by grammatical accident. Keying on `past` gets 27 of 29.

| `historical` F1 | development | held out |
|---|---:|---:|
| ConText + cues | 0.4444 | 0.2941 |
| **+ `past` header** | **0.6341** | **0.5250** |
| + defeasible | 0.6341 | 0.5063 |
| + `history`, naive | 0.4862 | 0.4252 |

On development the header arm added **15 true positives and zero false
positives**. Held out it is +0.231, the largest deterministic gain in this
effort.

**Two disclosures.** The defeater — a present-tense word in the span's own
sentence overriding the header — costs 0.019 and fires twice in 1,000 spans, both
times on spans that were correctly historical. It buys no *measured* protection.
It is nonetheless the whole difference between a cue and a prior, and the choice
between 0.5250 and 0.5063 is that choice, made explicitly rather than by default.

And the defeater list was wrong at first: it contained `presented`, `admitted`,
`started`, `developed` — past participles, in a word list meaning *this is
happening now*, and precisely the vocabulary a past-history section is full of.
They were removed on the grammar rather than on the score, but the held-out
figures had already been seen when that happened, so the sequence is recorded
here rather than the corrected number being presented as a first result.

## 6. What kind of concept carries a modifier

A per-dimension F1 treats every span as interchangeable. They are not. The 1,000
gold spans were drawn only from clinical semantic categories — `disorder`,
`finding`, `symptom`, `procedure`, `medication` — assigned by
`categories.categories_of` from UMLS semantic types **at sampling time, before
annotation**, so the mix is a property of the sample rather than a post-hoc
description of it.

Spans carry a broad and a narrow label where the hierarchy applies (`disease`
implies `disorder`, `diagnostic_procedure` implies `procedure`); the narrow one
is reported, so the table partitions the 1,000 spans exactly rather than
double-counting the 535 that are `disorder` in the wide sense.

| category | n | **modified** | `negated` | `uncertain` | `historical` | `family` | detector exactᵃ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| finding | 274 | 32.9% | 10.2% | 12.4% | 10.6% | 1.1% | 67.9% |
| medication | 201 | 17.4% | 3.0% | 10.0% | 4.5% | 0.0% | 79.1% |
| disease | 185 | **41.1%** | 8.6% | 14.6% | **17.8%** | 2.7% | 72.4% |
| procedure | 122 | 18.9% | **0.0%** | 10.7% | 8.2% | 0.0% | 76.2% |
| diagnostic procedure | 84 | 15.5% | 1.2% | 4.8% | 9.5% | 0.0% | **81.0%** |
| symptom | 76 | **61.8%** | **35.5%** | **25.0%** | 4.0% | 0.0% | **64.5%** |
| therapeutic procedure | 58 | 20.7% | 5.2% | 3.5% | 12.1% | 1.7% | 75.9% |

ᵃ share of spans where the shipped detector returns the **exact** gold label set.
Exact-set rather than per-dimension, because an operator anchored on a span with
the right negation and a spurious `historical` still edits the wrong cue.

> **Reading the first column: "modified", not "non-affirmed" and not
> "positive".**
>
> `modified` is the **union** of the four dimension columns — the span carries at
> least one modifier. Its complement is `unmodified`, which ConText and this
> codebase call `affirmed`. The dimension columns are the union's members, so the
> four do not sum to it: dimensions co-occur, and 12 of the 296 modified spans
> carry two (`no history of X` is both `negated` and `historical`). Across the
> full gold, 296 spans carry 308 label instances.
>
> **"Non-affirmed" was the earlier term and it is false for two of the four.**
> `historical` does not withhold the assertion — *history of hypertension*
> **affirms** the hypertension and places it in the past. `family` affirms the
> finding in someone else. Only `negated` genuinely withholds, and `uncertain`
> only partly. "Non-affirmed" is accurate only with the trailing qualifier *about
> the patient now*, which drops off every time the term is used alone. What all
> four actually do is **modify the asserted presence of the concept** — which is
> exactly what the SEMANTIC operators flip — so `modifier` is both positively
> defined and true of every member.
>
> **"Positive" is avoided for a different reason: it inverts between frames.**
> **Clinically**, *positive for pneumonia* means the patient has it — an
> *unmodified* span here. **In the scorer**, `gold_positives` for the `negated`
> dimension counts spans that *are* negated — clinically negative. The same word
> names opposite things one column apart.
>
> **Worked check, the `symptom` row.** A union is bounded by
> `max ≤ union ≤ sum`; here max 35.5%, sum 64.5%, union 61.8%. The 76 spans
> decompose as: 29 unmodified, 25 `negated` only, 19 `uncertain` only, 2
> `historical`+`negated`, 1 `historical` only. So `negated` = 25+2 = 27 (35.5%),
> `uncertain` = 19 (25.0%), `historical` = 2+1 = 3 (4.0%), and the union is
> 47/76 = 61.8% — the sum double-counts exactly the 2 spans with two labels.
>
> The union sits near the top of its range because on symptoms `negated` and
> `uncertain` are almost perfectly **disjoint**: not one symptom span is both
> denied and hedged. Symptoms attract two independent kinds of non-assertion —
> denials from the review of systems and diagnostic hedges — which is why they
> stack to 61.8% when no other category exceeds 41%.
>
> The overall rate — **29.6% of spans modified** — lands within half a point of
> the 30.1% of expert-annotated phenotype evidence that is not an affirmed
> statement about the patient now, in the SNOMED entity-linking gold. Two different corpora, two different annotation
> efforts, the same measure. That convergence is the reason this stage exists:
> collapsing `denies chest pain` into `severe chest pain` misreports roughly
> three spans in ten.

**Modifier load varies more than four-fold across concept types**, and the
variation is clinically legible rather than noise:

- **Symptoms are where modifiers live.** 61.8% modified and 35.5% negated —
  three times the corpus rate. This is the review of systems: symptoms are the
  things clinicians explicitly deny. A negation detector evaluated only on
  symptoms would look far better than one evaluated on a realistic mix, which is
  a reason to be careful reading any published negation number without its
  concept mix stated.
- **Procedures are never negated** — 0 of 122. A procedure is done or it is not
  mentioned; notes record *what happened*, not what did not. Their modifier mass
  is `uncertain` (planned) and `historical` (prior), which is what a temporality
  operator will find there.
- **Diseases carry the historical load** — 17.8%, the highest of any category, as
  past medical history is a list of diseases rather than symptoms.
- **`uncertain` on a medication is a different phenomenon from `uncertain` on a
  disease.** 10.0% of medications are uncertain, and they are almost entirely
  as-needed and conditional orders, not diagnostic hedging. The label is shared;
  the construction is not. This is the strongest argument for the merge in
  §"Four dimensions" — a lexicon splitting hedge from condition would have to
  split `PRN` from `possible`, and both are simply *not asserted as present now*.
- **`family` concentrates on diseases and findings** and never appears on
  medications or procedures, which matches how family history is written.

**Detector agreement tracks modifier density inversely.** 81.0% on diagnostic
procedures (15.5% modified) down to 64.5% on symptoms (61.8% modified).
The detector is most reliable exactly where there is least to detect, and the
pooled 73.3% figure averages over a 16.5-point spread. **A robustness dose
measured through this stage therefore carries different confidence depending on
which concepts it touched**, which is why `assertion_gold_profile.json` ships
beside the headline numbers rather than inside a footnote.

## 7. What the operators do with this

Detection is one instrument; **flipping is the other**. Injection–recovery: take
spans the detector marks with dimension *D*, apply the operator that flips *D*,
re-run the detector, and ask whether the attribute moved.

| operator | flip rate | collateral | eligible cues | spans flipped |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `semantic.temporality_shift` | **1.0000** | 0.0000 | 89 | 214 |
| `semantic.uncertainty_flip` | **1.0000** | 0.0020 | 153 | 301 |
| `semantic.negation_flip` | 0.9808 | 0.0087 | 490 | 717 |
| `semantic.experiencer_shift` | 0.8312 | 0.0043 | 48 | 64 |
| `semantic.temporality_shift_llm` | 0.9682 | 0.0003 | 89 | 152 |
| `semantic.uncertainty_flip_llm` | 0.9145 | 0.0007 | 153 | 214 |
| `semantic.negation_flip_llm` | 0.9163 | 0.0048 | 490 | 394 |
| `semantic.experiencer_shift_llm` | 0.7407 | 0.0036 | 48 | 40 |

*Run on `qualify_document` with the shipped sentence boundaries — the production
composition, not a hand-assembled one.*

Collateral is spans whose *other* dimensions moved, ≤0.87% everywhere. The
operators do the thing they claim to do, and they do it without spilling. Figures
are `validation/measurements/assertion_flip_recovery.json` (12 notes, severity
1.00); an earlier version of this table quoted a smaller run (392 negation cues,
flip 0.9762) that the committed artifact supersedes. The `_llm` rows carry the
fallback-to-table upper bound recorded in the artifact (0.12–0.61).

**The two instruments answer different questions and both are needed.** A flip
rate of 1.0 on `historical` means the operator reliably changes what the detector
reads — it does not mean the detector was reading correctly in the first place.
κ 0.312 says it often was not. An operator is exactly as trustworthy as the
detector that anchors it, and each operator carries its dimension's measured κ in
the taxonomy lockfile for that reason.

## 8. What is publishable

Honest positioning, dimension by dimension:

Best held-out F1 per dimension, by family, against a 0.70 bar:

| dimension | rules, start | rules, now | **best deterministic** | best measured | ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `negated` | 0.615 | 0.608 | **0.815** encoder | 0.970 | 0.941 |
| `uncertain` | 0.308 | 0.442 | **0.659** encoder | 0.886 | 0.895 |
| `historical` | 0.191 | **0.525** rules | **0.525** rules | 0.865 | 0.897 |
| `family` | — | — | — | — | — |

Read as claims:

| dimension | what the evidence supports |
|---|---|
| `negated` | **Strong, on three instruments.** F1 0.929 on published NegEx gold; on real notes 0.615 from rules, **0.815 from an encoder distilled from this gold**, 0.970 from a frontier LLM. Clears 0.70 deterministically. |
| `uncertain` | **Improved deterministically, and the residual is understood.** 0.308 → 0.442 by fixing cue direction, → 0.659 by distillation, against 0.886 measured. The rule ceiling is not vocabulary: 33 of 66 held-out misses have the cue *after* its target. |
| `historical` | **A genuinely open number, and rules hold it.** 0.191 → **0.525** via a section header treated as a cue, beating every fine-tuned encoder (best 0.410). **No published baseline exists anywhere**, because the i2b2 assertion task has no temporality class — whatever is reported here is the first figure of its kind. |
| `family` | **Not measurable at this sample size.** 4 held-out positives, 5 training. Prevalence, not performance, is the limit, and no number should be quoted. |

**A deterministic detector now clears 0.70 on one dimension of four, and the
other three have moved by 0.22–0.33.** That is the state of the package, stated
plainly.

What the three-family comparison adds is *why* each shortfall exists, which is
different in each case and not reducible to "the rules are old":

- `negated` was a **precision** problem that a distilled encoder solves.
- `uncertain` was a **scope-reach** problem that vocabulary could not fix.
- `historical` was a **structural-evidence** problem that no encoder trained on
  600 spans solves and a five-line rule does.

The LLM column is kept in every table as the achievable bound. It is not the
shipped detector — §5.5 — but omitting it would understate how much room remains,
and the gap is the honest uncertainty on any dose measured through this stage.

For a **robustness measurement** package this is still a reportable instrument:
the detector's own reliability is measured, disclosed, and attached to every dose
it denominates, and §6 shows that reliability varies by concept type as well. For
**clinical inference** on `uncertain` or `historical` it is not, and nothing here
should be read as claiming otherwise.

The gap between the detector and the annotator ceiling is not a defect to be
hidden before publication. It is the measurement, and stating it is what
distinguishes an instrument from a claim.

## Reproducing

```bash
# Instrument 1 — NegEx gold (fetches the corpus into data/, never committed)
python validation/pipeline/scripts/fetch_negex.py
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_accuracy.py --out validation/measurements/assertion_accuracy.json

# Instrument 2 — cue detection, held-out split
python validation/pipeline/scripts/cue_detection_eval.py --split held_out \
    --out validation/measurements/cue_detection.json

# Instrument 3 — span classification, held-out split
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_gold_eval.py --gold data/assertion_gold_mimic/gold.yaml \
    --corpus data/golden_notes.csv --split held_out \
    --out validation/measurements/assertion_gold_mimic_heldout.json

# Section 1 — why each error happened
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_error_analysis.py --split held_out --extended

# Section 5 — the encoder arm (needs the `neural` extra: pip install -e ".[neural]")
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_neural_eval.py

# Section 5 — the LLM arms. Needs OPENROUTER_API_KEY;
# every user supplies their own, none ships. Responses are cached, so a second
# run is offline and free.
python validation/pipeline/scripts/annotate_assertions.py --model google/gemini-3.6-flash \
    --prompt-version v2 --split held_out \
    --out data/assertion_gold_mimic/labels_36flash_v2_ho.json
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_llm_eval.py \
    --labels "gemini-3.6-flash/v2=data/assertion_gold_mimic/labels_36flash_v2_ho.json"

# Flip recovery
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_flip_recovery.py \
    --out validation/measurements/assertion_flip_recovery.json

# Section 5 — concept-category profile of the gold, counts only
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_gold_profile.py --corpus data/golden_notes.csv

# The synthetic worked examples in section 1
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_examples.py

# The real annotated MIMIC spans behind section 1.1 — writes to data/ only
python validation/pipeline/scripts/assertion_examples.py --gold data/assertion_gold_mimic/gold.yaml \
    --corpus data/golden_notes.csv --out data/assertion_gold_mimic/examples.json
```

The MIMIC gold, its raw annotations and the note corpus live in `data/` and are
never committed. Published artifacts carry counts, metrics and hashes only; the
scorer refuses to emit span surfaces when the gold declares `restricted: true`.
